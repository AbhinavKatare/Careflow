# Deterministic Draft Safety Checker: Known Gaps & Structural Limitations

**System Component:** Careflow Studio Deterministic Draft Checker  
**Module:** `server/pipeline/draft-check.ts`  
**Purpose:** Enforce non-negotiable safety guardrails on synthetic candidate drafts before human triage review.

---

## 1. Executive Architecture Summary

The deterministic draft checker acts as a **hard safety gate** in the Careflow Studio pipeline. Every candidate draft generated by an upstream model or template must satisfy six deterministic invariant checks before being presented to clinic reviewers:

1. **No Diagnosis Language:** Zero explicit diagnostic terminology, disease categorizations, or diagnostic assertions.
2. **No Treatment / Medication Advice:** Zero prescriptive orders, dosing adjustments, OTC drug suggestions, or regimen alterations.
3. **No External Action Claims:** Zero false claims that external communications, scheduling, or EHR write operations occurred (zero outbound actions exist).
4. **Fact Grounding & Traceability:** All dates, appointments, and clinical outcome claims must strictly trace to the participant's submission or context.
5. **Cross-Patient Data Isolation:** Zero references to other patient names, initials, or patient IDs.
6. **No Emergency Monitoring Implications:** Zero statements implying continuous vitals tracking, real-time telemetry, or automated 911 dispatch.

If any check fails, **the draft is blocked immediately** and the case routes to `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

---

## 2. Structural Gaps (What Deterministic Checking Cannot Catch)

Deterministic regex pattern-matching and token grounding provide a high-speed, reliable baseline safety floor. However, natural language generation possesses degrees of freedom that cannot be completely bounded by static pattern dictionaries.

The following represent known structural gaps:

### A. Creative Metaphors & Indirect Clinical Phrasing
- **Pattern:** Using non-clinical idiomatic speech to communicate a medical judgment.
- **Example:** *"It seems the engine in your chest is skipping a few gears"* instead of explicit clinical terms like `"arrhythmia"` or `"tachycardia"`.
- **Limitation:** Static lexicons target medical terminology, prefixes, and common synonyms; poetic or metaphorical framing can bypass keyword boundaries while still communicating a diagnostic opinion.

### B. Subtle Conversational Pragmatics & Sarcasm
- **Pattern:** Sarcastic reassurance, passive-aggressive encouragement, or double entendres.
- **Example:** *"Well, at least you managed to survive another routine week without calling us."*
- **Limitation:** Syntactically well-formed neutral words can carry negative affective tone or inappropriate bedside manner that cannot be flagged through simple phrase lists.

### C. Complex Implicit Counterfactuals & Causal Attribution
- **Pattern:** Implying medical causality without using overt prescribing or diagnostic words.
- **Example:** *"If you had skipped that morning beverage, your head wouldn't be pounding right now."*
- **Limitation:** Asserts a medical diagnosis and causal lifestyle link without using explicit prohibited medication or diagnostic keywords.

### D. Emergent Brand Names, Novel OTCs, and Street Slang
- **Pattern:** Emerging wellness supplements, proprietary OTC brand names, or slang terms for pharmaceuticals.
- **Example:** Recommending a novel herbal extract or niche nootropic by trademarked brand name not yet incorporated into the static `TREATMENT_PATTERNS` regex list.
- **Limitation:** Static dictionaries require versioned updates; newly marketed products or regional slang may evade detection until added to the spec.

### E. Multi-Session Temporal Hallucinations Outside Current Context
- **Pattern:** Referencing plausible past historical events not provided in the current single check-in payload.
- **Example:** *"Just like during your check-in last spring, take it easy."*
- **Limitation:** If historical sessions are not loaded into the context window, the checker cannot distinguish between true multi-session continuity and an ungrounded hallucination.

### F. Plausible Numerical & Activity Hallucinations
- **Pattern:** Fabricating non-critical numbers or activities that sound plausible.
- **Example:** Patient note states: *"I went for a light walk this afternoon."* Candidate draft states: *"Glad you were able to complete your 2-mile walk today!"*
- **Limitation:** The draft invents an exact distance (`"2-mile"`) that was not in the source text. While exact appointment dates and clinical outcomes are strictly grounded, generic colloquial numbers require full semantic entity extraction.

### G. Reassurance via Strategic Omission
- **Pattern:** The draft sounds completely polite, safe, and professional, but quietly ignores an acute symptom mentioned by the patient.
- **Example:** Patient states: *"Fever of 102 and dizziness, but routine is okay."* Draft responds: *"Thank you for your update, Demo Patient 001. We are glad your routine is on track!"*
- **Limitation:** The draft checker inspects the draft text itself for forbidden assertions. Catching symptom omission requires the authoritative triage policy engine, which routes the case to `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` before a draft is even prepared.

## 3. Defense-in-Depth Mitigation Strategy

Careflow Studio mitigates these structural gaps through an architecture of layered defenses:

```
[Untrusted Patient Submission]
       │
       ▼
[Pre-LLM Safety Layer] (Sanitization, Reversible Redaction, Injection Detection)
       │
       ▼
[Deterministic Policy Engine] (Authoritative Routing: Acute Symptoms -> HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED)
       │
       ├─► Route != DRAFT_FOR_REVIEW ──► [Draft Suppressed] ──► Human Review
       │
       ▼
[Candidate Draft Synthesis] (Advisory only)
       │
       ▼
[Deterministic Draft Safety Checker] (6 Invariant Checks with Offending Spans)
       │
       ├─► Check Fails ───────────────► [Draft Blocked] ───► Escalated to Staff Review
       │
       ▼ (Check Passes)
[Human Staff Review Queue] (Side-by-side diffing, audit logging, zero outbound actions)
```

1. **Routing Boundary:** The large language model never decides routing. Any acute symptom or free-text note containing distress is escalated to staff by deterministic TypeScript rules before drafting occurs.
2. **Reviewer Mandate:** Every draft prepared for review is explicitly simulated. No automated messages, SMS, or emails are ever dispatched.
3. **Structured Reviewer Actions:** Reviewers can edit, reject with structured reason codes (`inaccurate`, `tone`, `unsupported_claim`, `should-have-been-escalated`), and record edit distance for continuous quality improvement.
